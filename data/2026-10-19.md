Abstract: The frequency noise of an ordinary diode laser, often a nuisance in spectroscopy, can be used to measure atomic coherence. When atoms are prepared in a coherent superposition of two ground states, each random shift in laser frequency changes how much light they absorb, so the transmitted light carries a record of that coherence.
